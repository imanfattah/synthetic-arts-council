## Pack summary

- 9 registered sources

- 3 operational or normative arts-funder sources
- 2 UNESCO research or policy sources
- 2 foreign legal comparators

## What the pack supports

The sources support testing a **proportionate disclosure requirement** that distinguishes material creative uses from minor administrative assistance, keeps applicants accountable, asks about consent and rights, and does not automate grant decisions.

## What the pack does not establish

- that Jakarta artists support such a rule;
- that an existing Jakarta grant programme has authority or operational capacity to adopt it;
- that disclosure by itself resolves copyright, consent, compensation, bias, or labour harms;
- a settled Indonesian legal classification for AI-generated or AI-assisted work;
- the right definition of `material use`, enforcement process, or remedy.

## Known evidence imbalance

The pack is stronger on institutional and international policy than on direct Jakarta stakeholder evidence. The committee round may proceed as a **method test**, but it should be expected to request consultation before supporting real implementation.
